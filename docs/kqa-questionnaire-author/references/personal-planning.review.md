# Personal planning example: design and review record

**Status: illustrative draft only — NOT validated.** This artifact demonstrates a low-stakes, respondent-controlled personal reflection using survee's supported controls and bounded routing. It is not a validated instrument, clinical tool, diagnostic screen, treatment plan, coaching protocol, or measure of an individual's ability or worth. Do not use answers to make decisions about access, employment, eligibility, care, or other consequential outcomes.

## Scope and assumptions

- Intended use: an individual chooses whether to reflect on one personal goal or area of life and may record a small next step for their own use.
- Intended respondent: an adult who has chosen to take part in an informal personal-planning reflection. Suitability for minors, supported decision-making contexts, group settings, or any other population has not been assessed.
- The host should explain who will see responses, how they will be used, how long they will be retained, and how to ask questions or withdraw where applicable. This example makes no promise of confidentiality, anonymity, deletion, or a particular retention period.
- Every item is optional. Blank, “not sure,” and “prefer to keep the focus private” are not treated as deficits. A blank response is not interpreted as “none” or “not applicable.”
- Wording avoids asking for names, health details, or other sensitive information. Respondents are reminded to leave out details they do not want to share. Hosts should still review their collection context and provide a suitable notice before use.
- The instrument intentionally does not promise a specific completion time. **Target time: unmeasured**; measure through respondent testing before publishing an estimate.
- There is no scoring or ranking. The 1–5 answer is a present-moment self-check only; it must not be summed, benchmarked, interpreted as a trait, or used to compare people.
- The one routed detail question appears only for a substantive focus choice. If the respondent changes their focus to “keep the focus private” or clears it, the former answer is retained by survee while inactive and may reappear if the route becomes eligible again. The on-page disclosure and survee review flow explain that retained answers can be inspected and cleared.
- The example uses all-at-once presentation so respondents can move among sections with ordinary links. The package does not require a particular visual design or the use of routing.

## Decisions, question map, and interpretation boundaries

| Question ID | Decision or information need | Intended interpretation | Must not be inferred or used to decide |
|---|---|---|---|
| `focus-area` | Give the respondent an easy way to choose a topic or keep it private. | A self-selected topic for this reflection, at this time. | A stable preference, personal quality, need, or willingness to participate. |
| `focus-detail` | Let the respondent put the chosen topic into their own words. Shown only for the five substantive topic choices. | The respondent's own description of what they might like to make progress on. | A complete account, commitment, diagnosis, or permission to contact them. Avoid interpreting omitted or brief answers as lack of motivation. |
| `planning-horizon` | Offer a rough time frame for the respondent's reflection. | A planning frame they currently find useful, including uncertainty. | A deadline, promise, forecast, or externally imposed expectation. |
| `first-step` | Invite an optional small action idea. | A possible action the respondent chose to record. | Evidence that the action is feasible, completed, or obligatory. |
| `friction` | Surface circumstances the respondent thinks may make the step harder. | Up to three selected possibilities in this planning context. | A diagnosis, fixed barrier, personal shortcoming, or exhaustive list. A blank answer is not proof that no barriers exist. |
| `friction-other` | Allow a short optional explanation when “Something else” is selected. | Only the circumstance the respondent chose to write down. | A complete explanation or authorization to investigate further. It becomes inactive if “Something else” is unselected; the saved text may be retained until cleared in Review answers. |
| `helpful-support` | Invite respondent-selected ideas that could make the step easier. | Up to three ideas the respondent might find useful. | A request for the host or another person to provide support, unless separately agreed. A blank answer is not a deficiency. |
| `step-fit` | Provide a brief self-check on how manageable the proposed step feels right now. | A single subjective response for this moment. | A validated scale, clinical signal, measure of capability, persistent state, or basis for comparison. |
| `adjustment` | Give the respondent a final chance to revise, reduce, defer, or leave the plan. | Optional changes the respondent wants to record. | Agreement to follow a plan or a reason for any change. |

## Validation checklist

This is an example review aid, not evidence that the instrument is valid or ready for deployment. It is **illustrative and NOT validated**.

- [ ] Confirm the intended use, audience, consequences, response access, and host notice are accurate for the actual deployment.
- [ ] Complete respondent cognitive pretesting and record item-level findings; revise ambiguous, sensitive, or burdensome wording.
- [ ] Conduct a separate usability and accessibility review, including keyboard and assistive technology, mobile reflow, and mixed-language shell considerations.
- [ ] Verify routing, hidden-answer retention and clearing, answer save/restore, review, export, submission, and locked-state behavior in the live app.
- [ ] Check that any time estimate is based on observed respondent data; it is currently **unmeasured**.
- [ ] Reassess local privacy, safeguarding, consent, and records requirements. This template does not settle them.
- [ ] Reconfirm that responses will not be scored, used diagnostically, used to compare people, or used for consequential decisions.
